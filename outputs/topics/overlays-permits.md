# Overlays & Permit Triggers

## AI Summary
Overlays sit above zone provisions and can independently trigger a planning permit or add design requirements beyond ResCode. Must be identified at project inception using council's planning map or SPEAR. Permit triggers also arise from Clause 52 particular provisions regardless of zone or overlay. Always check all overlays and Clause 52 before advising whether a permit is needed.

## Common Council / Project Issues
- HO: councils require a heritage impact assessment (HIA) for any external works or demolition; contributory vs significant classification affects the test applied
- DDO: schedules vary widely — some DDOs have prescriptive controls (max heights, setback tables), others are outcome-based; always read the specific schedule number
- NCO: neighbourhood character overlays are the most subjective; councils often cite NCO schedules in VicSmart refusals; material and design justification is key
- VPO: arborist reports are required before clearing or works within tree protection zones; exemptions by species and size are listed in the schedule
- Flood (LSIO/FO): habitable floor levels must be at or above flood level + 300mm (or council-specified level); structural engineer report may be required
- Bushfire (BMO/BSMO): CFA provides a report; BAL rating determines AS 3959 construction standard; NCC Section C applies at building permit stage
- Restrictive covenants: must be checked on title; variation requires VCAT application; "single dwelling" covenants are common in older estates and affect subdivision

## What to Check
- Run the subject address through SPEAR or council's online planning map — list ALL overlays present
- For each overlay: check Section 1 (permit exempt) vs Section 2 (permit required) in the schedule
- Heritage (HO): confirm whether place is significant or contributory; read the heritage citation; check council's local heritage policy (Cl 43.01-5)
- DDO: read the specific schedule for height limits, setback controls, materials, and decision guidelines — do not assume standard ResCode applies
- NCO: identify preferred or prohibited materials, roof forms, and street setback preferences from the schedule
- VPO: identify exempt species and sizes; determine if works are within TPZ or SRZ of protected trees
- Flood: obtain flood level from council or Melbourne Water; calculate required habitable floor level
- Bushfire: confirm BAL rating from CFA; check if site is in BMO and/or BSMO; obtain CFA report for permit application
- Cl 52 triggers: check Cl 52.06 (parking), Cl 52.17 (native vegetation), Cl 52.34 (bicycle), Cl 52.29 (roads), Cl 52.18 (industry — not typical)
- Title search: obtain current title and check for covenants, easements, and restrictions before commencing design

## Draft Response Logic
**HO RFI — external works:** Respond with a heritage impact statement addressing: (1) the heritage citation and significance statement, (2) how the design responds to heritage character (scale, materials, setback, visibility), (3) whether demolition or alteration affects any fabric with significance. Refer to the Burra Charter principles where relevant.

**DDO query:** Confirm which DDO schedule applies and the specific requirement being addressed. State whether the design meets, varies, or seeks to exercise the decision guidelines, and provide design justification addressing the stated objectives of the DDO.

**Flood level query:** Provide the adopted flood level (source: Melbourne Water or council), the proposed habitable floor level (FFL), and confirm the 300mm freeboard. Attach the engineer's structural advice if required.

**Covenant question:** Advise that covenants are private legal instruments, not planning controls, and confirm that a VCAT application under s84 of the Property Law Act is the mechanism for variation — not a planning permit.
